About Compound Wealth Calc

Compound Wealth Calc makes investing feel less like homework and more like a game. It's built and written by one person: me, Aaron Eason.

Aaron EasonCreator of Compound Wealth Calc. I build the calculators, write every guide and check the math behind each example.

I started Compound Wealth Calc because most retirement calculators felt like tax forms. You typed in numbers and got one big total that was hard to believe. I wanted something that showed how money grows: what part came from you, what part came from growth, and the age when work could become a choice.

So I built Freedom Quest. You pick a real fund, choose how much of each paycheck to invest, and watch your money hit milestones on the way to your "Freedom Day." Then I added tools to compare funds side by side and to see what savings accounts and CDs really earn.

How I write the guides

  • Plain English. I aim for about a 7th-grade reading level. If a term is needed, I explain it the first time it shows up.
  • Real math, shown. Every example on the site is worked out with the same formulas the calculators use. I don't copy numbers from other sites.
  • Official sources. Tax limits and rules come from the IRS. Fund fees and returns come from the fund companies. I link sources at the end of each guide.
  • Honest about risk. Past returns aren't promises, and I say so. When a fund has grown fast, I also show how hard it has fallen.
  • Kept up to date. Each guide shows the date it was last updated. When limits or rates change, I update the pages that use them.

How the site makes money

Compound Wealth Calc is free to use. It may show ads from Google. No fund company pays me to mention its funds, and ads never change what a calculator shows or what a guide says.

Where the numbers come from

Fund returns are rounded 10-year averages. Tax figures use 2026 IRS rules. Savings and CD rates are dated so you know how fresh they are. The How we calculate page explains every step.
