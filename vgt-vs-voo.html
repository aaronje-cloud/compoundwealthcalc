VGT vs VOO: which is better for you?

Updated November 16, 2026 · 5 min read · By Aaron Eason

VGT owns only U.S. technology companies. VOO owns about 500 big U.S. companies in every industry, including those same tech giants.

The short answer

VGT has grown much faster over the last 10 years because tech led the market. But it's one industry, and it has had much bigger drops. VOO already holds a big slice of tech, plus everything else.

What is VGT?

VGT owns only U.S. technology companies, big and small. A few giant tech companies make up a large share of it.

What is VOO?

VOO follows the S&P 500, a list of about 500 of the biggest U.S. companies across every industry. It's one of the cheapest and most popular ways to own the U.S. stock market.

Risk

VGT's typical swings are very high, and VOO's are medium. Funds that hold fewer kinds of companies tend to rise and fall more. The best fund is one you can hold through a bad year without selling.

Which one fits you?

  • Pick VGT if you want extra tech on top of a broad fund, you'll invest for many years, and you can sit through big drops.
  • Pick VOO if you want one fund that covers the whole U.S. economy, tech included.

Compare them

You already own a lot of tech in VOO

Tech is the biggest part of the S&P 500, about a third of VOO. Apple, Microsoft and Nvidia sit at the top of both funds. So VOO isn't "no tech." It's tech plus health care, banks, energy, stores, factories and more.

VGT is 100% tech. It owns those same giants in much bigger amounts, along with hundreds of smaller tech companies.

Surprise: VGT doesn't own Amazon, Alphabet (Google) or Meta (Facebook). The official industry lists put them in "consumer" and "communication" groups, not tech. VOO owns all three.

Why the 30-year numbers look so wild

The growth table shows VGT turning $200 a paycheck into millions. That's what happens when you assume tech keeps growing over 20% a year for 30 years. It almost certainly won't. No single industry has done that for that long. Use these numbers to see how returns compound, not to plan your retirement.

What happens in a bad year

Tech funds fall harder. In 2022, VGT dropped about 30% while VOO dropped about 18%. In the dot-com crash of 2000 to 2002, tech stocks lost far more than that. One-industry funds can go years without a gain.

A common way to use both

Many investors keep VOO (or VTI) as their main fund and add a smaller slice of VGT if they want more tech. That keeps the core spread across the whole economy. Just remember that the tech slice doubles up on companies VOO already owns.

Quick answers

Is VGT better than VOO?

VGT has grown faster over the last 10 years because tech led. But it holds only one industry and drops harder. VOO is more balanced.

Which has lower fees, VGT or VOO?

VGT charges 0.09% a year and VOO charges 0.03%. VOO is cheaper.

Which pays more dividends, VGT or VOO?

VOO has paid about 1.3% a year and VGT about 0.6%. Yields change over time.

Does VOO include tech stocks?

Yes. Tech is the biggest part of the S&P 500, about a third of VOO.

Should I own VGT and VOO together?

Some people do to tilt toward tech. It doubles up on the same big companies, so keep the tech slice to an amount you're comfortable losing a lot of in a bad year.
