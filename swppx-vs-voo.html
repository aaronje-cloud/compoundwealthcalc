SWPPX vs VOO: which is better for you?

Updated November 16, 2026 · 5 min read · By Aaron Eason

SWPPX and VOO both follow the S&P 500. One is a mutual fund from Schwab and the other is an ETF from Vanguard. They own the same companies.

The short answer

They're nearly identical. SWPPX charges a bit less (0.02% vs 0.03%) and is easy to auto-invest at Schwab. VOO works at almost any broker and is usually a little better for taxes in a regular account.

What is SWPPX?

SWPPX is Schwab's S&P 500 mutual fund. It has no minimum to invest at Schwab and one of the lowest fees of any fund. Like all mutual funds, it trades once a day, after the market closes.

What is VOO?

VOO follows the S&P 500, a list of about 500 of the biggest U.S. companies across every industry. It's one of the cheapest and most popular ways to own the U.S. stock market.

Risk

SWPPX's typical swings are medium, and VOO's are medium. Funds that hold fewer kinds of companies tend to rise and fall more. The best fund is one you can hold through a bad year without selling.

Which one fits you?

  • Pick SWPPX if you invest at Schwab and want to auto-invest exact dollar amounts every payday.
  • Pick VOO if you use another broker, might switch brokers later, or invest in a regular taxable account.

A little history

Schwab started SWPPX in 1997 as its low-cost S&P 500 fund. Vanguard launched VOO in 2010. Over the years, both companies kept cutting fees to win customers. Today SWPPX charges 0.02% and VOO charges 0.03%. On $10,000, that's $2 a year versus $3.

If you're putting money in every paycheck, the "once a day" thing doesn't matter. You're buying for the next 30 years, not the next hour.

Taxes in a regular account

In a 401(k), IRA or Roth IRA, taxes on the fund don't matter while your money is inside. In a regular brokerage account, they can. ETFs like VOO are built in a way that usually lets them avoid paying out capital gains to owners. Mutual funds sometimes have to sell shares and pass the gains to you, which means a tax bill even if you didn't sell anything.

S&P 500 index funds trade very little, so these payouts are usually small or zero. Still, for a taxable account, VOO has a small edge.

What if I leave Schwab?

You can usually move SWPPX to another broker as-is, without selling. But some brokers charge a fee to buy more Schwab mutual funds, and some don't offer them at all. VOO can be bought for free almost anywhere. If you think you might switch brokers someday, an ETF like VOO keeps things simpler.

Other cheap S&P 500 choices

Fidelity's FXAIX charges 0.015% and works like SWPPX, but at Fidelity. See VOO vs FXAIX. iShares' IVV is an ETF that charges 0.03%, like VOO. All of them own the same 500 companies.

Quick answers

Is SWPPX better than VOO?

They own the same S&P 500 companies. SWPPX is a bit cheaper and easy to auto-invest at Schwab. VOO is easier to buy anywhere and usually better for taxes in a regular account.

Which has lower fees, SWPPX or VOO?

SWPPX charges 0.02% a year and VOO charges 0.03%. SWPPX is cheaper.

Which pays more dividends, SWPPX or VOO?

Both pay about 1.2% to 1.3% a year, since they own the same companies. Yields change over time.

Can I buy SWPPX outside of Schwab?

Some brokers offer it, but they may charge a fee to buy it. It's simplest at Schwab.

Is SWPPX good for a Roth IRA?

Yes, many people use it there. Inside a Roth IRA, the tax difference between mutual funds and ETFs doesn't matter.
