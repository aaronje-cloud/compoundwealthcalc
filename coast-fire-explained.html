Coast FIRE explained: when can you stop saving for retirement?

Updated October 30, 2026 · 5 min read · By Aaron Eason

Coast FIRE means you've saved enough that your money can grow to your retirement goal on its own. You still work to pay today's bills, but you don't need to save for retirement anymore.

Key points

  • Your "coast number" is what you need invested today to reach your goal by retirement age.
  • The younger you are, the smaller your coast number.
  • After you hit it, you could take a lower-stress job or work fewer hours.

The simple idea

Say you need $1.25 million by age 65. If you have enough invested at 30, compound growth could get you there by 65 without adding another dollar. That starting amount is your coast number.

Coast number = retirement goal ÷ (1 + yearly return) ^ years until retirement

Coast numbers by age

To reach $1.25 million by 65, here's what you'd need invested today:

Many people use 7% because it's closer to stock returns after inflation. That keeps the goal in today's dollars.

Coast FIRE vs other kinds of FIRE

  • Full FIRE: enough saved to stop working completely.
  • Coast FIRE: enough saved for later, but you still work for today's costs.
  • Barista FIRE: you've saved a lot and use a part-time job to fill the gap.
  • Lean FIRE: retire on a small budget.

Why people like Coast FIRE

  • You can switch to work you enjoy, even if it pays less.
  • Less pressure to save every dollar.
  • It's a reachable goal much earlier in life.

Things to watch

  • Returns aren't promised. A slow decade could leave you short. Many people keep saving a little anyway.
  • Don't touch the money. Pulling money out breaks the plan.
  • Health insurance. A lower-paying job may have weaker benefits.

A worked example

Jordan is 30 and has $100,000 invested. Jordan's goal is $1 million by age 65.

  • If the money grows 7% a year with no new deposits, it could reach about $1,067,000 by 65. Jordan has reached Coast FIRE.
  • If the money only grows 5% a year, it would reach about $552,000. That's barely half the goal.

The gap between those two lines is the biggest risk of Coast FIRE. A small change in the return you plan for makes a huge change in the result after 35 years. Being careful with your guess matters.

How to coast safely

  1. Plan with a careful return. Use 5% to 7% after fees, not the best 10 years you've ever seen.
  2. Add a cushion. Many people wait until they have 10% to 25% more than their coast number before slowing down.
  3. Don't stop completely. Even $100 a month, or just getting your 401(k) match, gives you a safety margin.
  4. Check once a year. If a bad market pushes you behind, you can add more for a while to catch up.
  5. Keep an emergency fund. Coasting usually means less income. Cash keeps you from selling investments when something breaks.

What does coasting look like in real life?

People who reach their coast number use the freedom in different ways:

  • Moving from a high-stress job to one they enjoy more, even if it pays less.
  • Working part-time or four days a week.
  • Taking a year to raise kids, care for a parent or go back to school.
  • Starting a small business without needing it to pay big right away.

The key rule is that your income still covers all your bills today. Coasting means you stop saving for retirement, not that you start spending your retirement money.

Coast FIRE and inflation

If you plan with a return that's already "after inflation," like 5%, your goal stays in today's dollars. If you plan with a regular return like 7% or 8%, remember that $1 million in 35 years will buy much less than $1 million today. Mixing those two up is one of the most common mistakes in Coast FIRE math.

Find your own coast number in three steps

  1. Pick your goal. Multiply the yearly spending you want in retirement by 25. For $40,000 a year, that's $1,000,000.
  2. Count the years. Subtract your age from the age you want to retire. If you're 35 and want to retire at 65, that's 30 years.
  3. Work backward. Divide your goal by how much $1 grows in that many years. At 7% a year, $1 grows to about $7.61 in 30 years. So $1,000,000 ÷ 7.61 = about $131,000.

If you already have $131,000 invested at 35, you could stop adding to retirement and still be on track for $1 million at 65, if returns hold up. Not there yet? That's normal. Most people reach their coast number somewhere in their 30s or 40s. Every dollar you invest now brings that day closer.

Quick answers

What is Coast FIRE?

It means you have enough invested that, without adding more, it should grow to your retirement goal by your planned retirement age.

How do I calculate my Coast FIRE number?

Divide your retirement goal by (1 + your expected yearly return) raised to the number of years until retirement.

Is Coast FIRE realistic?

It can be, especially if you start investing in your 20s. Many people still save a little after reaching it, just to be safe.

What return should I use for Coast FIRE?

Many people use about 7%, which is close to long-run stock returns after inflation.
