What is Coast FIRE?

Updated October 30, 2026 · By Aaron Eason

FIRE stands for financial independence, retire early. Full FIRE means your investments can already pay for your life. Coast FIRE is a halfway point. You've saved enough that, even if you never add another dollar, compound growth could carry your money to your full goal by retirement age. You still work to pay today's bills, but you're done saving for retirement.

The Coast FIRE formula

Coast number = retirement goal ÷ (1 + yearly return)years until retirement

Example: you want to spend $40,000 a year, so your goal is $40,000 × 25 = $1,000,000. You're 30 and want to retire at 60, so you have 30 years. At 7% a year, $1 grows to about $7.61 in 30 years. $1,000,000 ÷ 7.61 = about $131,000. That's your coast number today.

How to read your results

  • Coast number today is what you'd need invested right now to stop saving.
  • Your Coast FIRE age is when your balance, plus your monthly deposits, catches up to the coast line. The coast line rises each year, because you have less time left for growth.
  • If you stop now shows what today's balance alone could grow to by your retirement age.
  • The chart shows the gold coast line, your plan (invest until you cross it, then stop) and what happens if you keep investing all the way.

Tips for planning safely

  • Plan with a careful return. If 5% still works, you're in good shape.
  • Add a cushion. Many people wait until they're 10% to 25% past their coast number before cutting back.
  • Keep getting any 401(k) match, even after you coast. It's free money.
  • Check once a year. A bad market can push you below the line for a while.

Quick answers

What is my Coast FIRE number?

It's the amount you need invested today so that, with no more deposits, it grows to your full retirement goal by your retirement age. The formula is: goal ÷ (1 + yearly return) raised to the number of years left.

What return should I use?

Many people plan with 5% to 7% a year. If you use a return that's already after inflation, like 5%, your goal stays in today's dollars. Using the best past 10 years can make the result look much rosier than it may turn out.

What happens after I reach Coast FIRE?

You can stop adding to retirement savings and only earn enough to cover today's bills. Many people keep investing a little anyway, or at least get their 401(k) match, as a safety cushion.

Is Coast FIRE the same as retiring early?

No. With Coast FIRE you still work and pay your own bills. You've just finished saving for retirement. Full FIRE means your investments already cover all your spending today.
