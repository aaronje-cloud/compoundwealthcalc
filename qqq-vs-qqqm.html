QQQ vs QQQM: which is better for you?

Updated October 30, 2026 · 5 min read · By Aaron Eason

QQQ and QQQM follow the exact same Nasdaq-100 list. So what's the difference?

The short answer

For long-term investors, QQQM is usually the better choice because it costs less each year (0.15% vs 0.18%). QQQ trades much more each day, which matters mostly to traders.

What is QQQ?

QQQ follows the Nasdaq-100: the 100 biggest non-financial companies on the Nasdaq. It leans heavily on tech. It's very popular with traders because it trades so much each day.

What is QQQM?

QQQM follows the same Nasdaq-100 list as QQQ, at a lower fee. It started in 2020, so its 10-year number uses the index's record.

Risk

QQQ's typical swings are high, and QQQM's are high. Funds that hold fewer kinds of companies tend to rise and fall more. The best fund is one you can hold through a bad year without selling.

Which one fits you?

  • Pick QQQ if you trade often or use options.
  • Pick QQQM if you're buying and holding for years and want the lower fee.

Why two funds for the same list?

QQQ started in 1999. For years it was one of the most traded funds in the world, and traders loved it. In 2020, Invesco launched QQQM as a version built for people who buy and hold. Same 100 companies, a lower fee and a lower share price.

In December 2025, QQQ's owners voted to turn QQQ into a standard ETF, the same kind of fund as QQQM. Its fee dropped from 0.20% to 0.18%. That made the two funds more alike than ever. Today the main differences are the fee, the share price and how much each one trades.

I already own QQQ. Should I switch?

It depends on where you own it.

  • In a 401(k), IRA or Roth IRA: switching doesn't cause taxes. If you want the lower fee, you can sell QQQ and buy QQQM the same day.
  • In a regular brokerage account: selling can cause a tax bill. It's usually better to keep the QQQ you have and send new money to QQQM.

Here's why. Say you own $20,000 of QQQ that you bought for $12,000. Selling it means an $8,000 gain. At a 15% capital gains rate, that's about $1,200 in tax. Switching saves you 0.03% a year, or about $6 a year on $20,000. It would take a very, very long time to earn back that $1,200.

What about the share price?

A lower share price used to matter when you had to buy whole shares. If QQQ costs hundreds of dollars a share, $200 a paycheck might buy zero shares. Today most big brokers sell fractional shares, so you can put in any dollar amount in either fund. If your broker doesn't offer fractional shares, QQQM's lower price makes it easier to invest small amounts.

Both funds have the same risk

Picking QQQM over QQQ saves on fees. It doesn't make the fund safer. Both hold the same 100 companies, which lean heavily on big tech. In 2022, the Nasdaq-100 fell about a third. Before you choose either one, read VOO vs QQQM to decide whether the Nasdaq-100 fits you at all.

Quick answers

Is QQQ better than QQQM?

For long-term investors, QQQM is usually the better choice because it costs less each year (0.15% vs 0.18%). QQQ trades much more each day, which matters mostly to traders.

Which has lower fees, QQQ or QQQM?

QQQ charges 0.18% a year and QQQM charges 0.15%. QQQM is cheaper.

Which pays more dividends, QQQ or QQQM?

QQQ has paid about 0.6% a year and QQQM about 0.6%. Yields change over time.

Why does QQQM have a lower fee?

Invesco created QQQM in 2020 as a cheaper version for long-term investors.
