How we calculate

Last updated October 30, 2026

Here is exactly how our calculators turn your numbers into results, where our data comes from, and what the results leave out.

Fund returns

  • Each fund uses its average yearly return over about 10 years (roughly mid-2016 to mid-2026). This is the steady yearly rate that would turn the starting value into the ending value.
  • Total return assumes dividends are reinvested. Price return leaves dividends out. The calculator uses total return when dividend reinvesting is on and price return when it's off.
  • Figures are rounded from each fund company's published performance data (such as Vanguard, Schwab, Fidelity, Invesco and State Street). We last reviewed them in October 2026.
  • Fund returns are already counted after the fund's yearly fee, so we don't subtract the fee a second time.

Paycheck investing and compounding

You can invest monthly (12 times a year), every two weeks (26 times) or weekly (52 times). We turn the yearly return into a per-paycheck rate:

per-paycheck rate = (1 + yearly return)1 ÷ paychecks per year − 1

Each paycheck, your balance grows by that rate and then your new deposit is added. This way, 12 monthly steps or 26 biweekly steps add up to exactly the yearly return. If you pick a percent of your paycheck, we multiply your paycheck by that percent to get the deposit.

Dividends

Each fund has an approximate dividend yield. When reinvesting is on, dividends buy more shares and are part of the total return. When it's off, we show the cash dividends you'd receive and assume you spend them, so they don't grow.

Your Freedom number and Freedom Day

  • Your Freedom number is your yearly spending times 25. This comes from the 4% rule: taking out about 4% a year has lasted at least 30 years in most past markets.
  • Your Freedom Day is the first paycheck when your balance reaches that number.
  • If you set a retire-by age, we also show how much per paycheck it would take to get there in time.

Taxes by account type

The tax comparison uses 2026 federal rules: the standard deduction ($16,100 single, $32,200 married filing jointly), the 2026 income tax brackets and the long-term capital gains brackets (0% up to $49,450 single or $98,900 married). It does not include state taxes, the extra 3.8% investment tax on high incomes, or penalties beyond the basic 10% early withdrawal rule. Contribution limits use 2026 IRS numbers: $24,500 for a 401(k), $7,500 for an IRA, plus catch-up amounts for people 50 and older.

Savings and CD calculator

Rates are shown as APY (annual percentage yield). You can choose how often interest compounds: daily, monthly, quarterly, twice a year or yearly. Monthly deposits are added at the end of each month. Example rates on the page are dated so you can see how fresh they are; real rates change often.

What the results don't include

  • Ups and downs. Results grow smoothly each year. Real markets jump around, and the order of good and bad years matters, especially right after you retire.
  • Inflation. The growth chart is in future dollars. Some guides use a return "after inflation" and say so when they do.
  • Taxes on the growth chart. Only the account comparison section looks at taxes.
  • Pay raises. Deposits stay the same unless you change them.

Checking our work

The examples in our guides are worked out with the same formulas the calculators use. If you find a number that looks wrong, please tell us and we'll fix it and update the date on the page.
