VT vs VTI: which is better for you?

Updated November 16, 2026 · 5 min read · By Aaron Eason

VT owns the whole world's stock market. VTI owns just the U.S. market. VT already includes almost everything in VTI.

The short answer

VTI has grown faster over the last 10 years because U.S. stocks led. VT spreads your money worldwide in one simple fund.

What is VT?

VT owns stocks from the whole world in one fund, about 60% U.S. and 40% other countries. It's a true one-fund portfolio.

What is VTI?

VTI owns nearly the whole U.S. stock market: big, medium and small companies. Because big companies make up most of its value, it moves a lot like the S&P 500.

Risk

VT's typical swings are medium, and VTI's are medium. Funds that hold fewer kinds of companies tend to rise and fall more. The best fund is one you can hold through a bad year without selling.

Which one fits you?

  • Pick VT if you want one fund that covers every country.
  • Pick VTI if you want just the U.S. market, or plan to add an international fund yourself.

VT is basically VTI plus the rest of the world. About 60% of VT is U.S. stocks, the same companies VTI owns. The other 40% is companies in Japan, Europe, Canada, China, India and other countries.

Build your own VT for less

You can copy VT with two funds: about 60% VTI and 40% VXUS (Vanguard's international fund). That mix costs about 0.038% a year, a bit less than VT's 0.06%. On $100,000, that's about $38 a year instead of $60.

The catch: you have to keep the mix in balance yourself, and decide how much to put in each one every payday. VT does all of that for you automatically. For many people, that simplicity is worth $20 a year. See VTI vs VXUS for the two-fund way.

Has the U.S. always beaten the world?

No. The last 10 years were great for U.S. stocks, which is why VTI is ahead in the tables above. But from 2000 to 2009, the U.S. market ended the decade slightly lower than it started, while many international markets did better. Leadership tends to switch back and forth over time, and nobody can predict when.

A tax perk in regular accounts

Other countries tax the dividends VT gets from foreign companies. In a regular brokerage account, you can usually claim a credit for those taxes on your U.S. tax return, so you don't lose that money. In an IRA or 401(k), you can't claim it. It's a small thing, but it's one reason some people hold international funds in taxable accounts.

Quick answers

Is VT better than VTI?

VTI has grown faster over the last 10 years because U.S. stocks led. VT spreads your money worldwide in one simple fund.

Which has lower fees, VT or VTI?

VT charges 0.06% a year and VTI charges 0.03%. VTI is cheaper.

Which pays more dividends, VT or VTI?

VT has paid about 2.0% a year and VTI about 1.3%. Yields change over time.

Is VT enough by itself?

Many people use VT as a one-fund portfolio, since it owns thousands of companies worldwide.

Does VT include VTI?

VT owns the same U.S. companies VTI does, making up about 60% of VT. The rest is companies outside the U.S.

Should I own VT and VTI together?

You can, but it tilts you more toward the U.S. Many people pick one: VT for the whole world, or VTI plus an international fund to set their own mix.
